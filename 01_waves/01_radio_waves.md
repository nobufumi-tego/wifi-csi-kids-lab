# 1-1. Radio waves — invisible waves all around you

Right now, invisible waves are passing through the room you are in. On this page you
will find out what a wave is, and why Wi-Fi is one of them.

## Waves you already know

Drop a small stone into a still pond. Rings spread out from where it landed. The water
does not travel across the pond: each spot just goes up and down. What travels is the
**shape**. That moving shape is a **wave**.

```text
   ~   ~   ~   ~        rings move outward
 ~   ~   o   ~   ~      o = where the stone fell
   ~   ~   ~   ~
```

- **Sound** is a wave in the air. Your voice pushes the air a little, and that push
  travels to someone's ear.
- **Light** is a wave too.
- **Radio waves** are the same kind of wave as light, but with a much longer
  wavelength (you will measure it on the next page), so our eyes cannot see them.

## Radio waves around you

| Thing | Uses radio waves to… |
|---|---|
| Wi-Fi | connect phones and computers to the internet |
| Bluetooth | connect wireless earphones or a game controller |
| TV and radio broadcasts | send programs from a tower to your home |
| Mobile phones | talk to the nearest phone tower |
| GPS | receive signals from satellites to find your position |

Radio waves travel at the **speed of light**: about 300,000 km per second. That is
fast enough to go around the Earth about 7 times in one second.

## What happens when radio waves meet things

When a radio wave hits something, three things can happen:

1. part of it **bounces off** (reflection),
2. part of it **goes through**,
3. part of it is **absorbed** (turned into a tiny bit of heat).

Metal bounces radio waves very well. People are mostly water, and water both absorbs
and bounces them. This is why **a person in the room changes the Wi-Fi signal**, and
it is the starting point of everything in this lab.
