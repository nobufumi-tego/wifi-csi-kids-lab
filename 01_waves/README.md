# Chapter 1: Waves — how radio travels

**Goal:** understand what a radio wave is, and why the same Wi-Fi signal can arrive
strong in one place and weak in another. By the end you will know the one idea that
makes Wi-Fi sensing work.

**Time:** about 1 hour (4 short pages + an optional notebook)

**What you need:** just curiosity. The Python parts are optional. To try them, start
the lab first (`./start.sh`, double-click `start.bat`, or `uv run lab.py`; see the
[home page](../README.md)).

## Pages

| # | Page | What you will find out | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-1 | [Radio waves](01_radio_waves.md) | What a wave is, and where radio waves are around you | 10 min |
| 1-2 | [Wavelength and frequency](02_wavelength_and_frequency.md) | How long one Wi-Fi wave is (about 12.5 cm) | 15 min |
| 1-3 | [Reflection and paths](03_reflection_and_paths.md) | Waves bounce, so they travel along many paths | 15 min |
| 1-4 | [Adding waves (interference)](04_interference.md) | Waves help or cancel each other, and a moving person changes that | 20 min |
