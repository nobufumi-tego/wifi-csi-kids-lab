# For parents and teachers

## What children will do

This lab teaches Wi-Fi sensing: measuring how Wi-Fi radio waves change when people
move. Children work through five levels:

| Level | Activity | Time (rough guide) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Waves | Ideas about radio waves, reflection, and interference; small thought experiments | 1–2 hours |
| 2 CSI basics | Install Python tools and draw pretend (simulated) CSI data | 1–3 hours |
| 3 Build | Flash firmware to two ESP32 boards and record real data | 2–4 hours (with an adult) |
| 4 Analysis | Compute a "motion number" and choose a threshold | 2–3 hours |
| 5 Machine learning | Train and test a simple classifier | 2–4 hours |

Times vary a lot with age and experience. Levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 need only a computer.
A free-research project (自由研究) usually adds several sessions of planning,
measuring, and writing.

## What it costs

- A computer (Windows, macOS, Linux, or ChromeOS with Linux enabled) that can install software.
- For Level 3 only: **two ESP32 development boards** whose radio module shows the
  **技適 mark**, and **two USB cables that carry data** (some cables only charge).
- All software used here is free.

## Where adults should help

- **Installing software** (uv, drivers, Arduino IDE or PlatformIO) and approving permissions.
- **Buying parts.** Check for the 技適 mark. See the [safety guide](safety.md).
- **Electricity.** USB power only. Unplug the boards after use.
- **Privacy.** Make sure everyone in the room agrees to be recorded, and keep
  recordings on the family computer. Wi-Fi sensing can reveal what people are doing,
  so consent matters.
- **AI tools.** Each AI service has its own terms of use, **including age requirements**.
  Please check the terms of the service you plan to use, set up the account yourself
  if needed, and use it together with your child.

## Learning with AI

The file [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) asks AI coding tools to act as a tutor. It asks
the AI to give hints before answers, keep safety and privacy rules, and not write the
child's report. AI can still make mistakes. Encourage children to check answers by
running code and comparing with the lessons. See
[Learning with AI](learning-with-ai.md).

## Fitting it into free research (自由研究)

- Value the child's **own question, prediction, and reasoning** more than a polished
  result. "It didn't work, and here is why I think so" is a good outcome.
- Ask children to keep a short **AI use log** (what they asked, what they learned,
  what they checked themselves). It makes AI use transparent and easy to discuss.
- Pretend (simulated) data is useful for practice, but results should say clearly
  whether they come from simulation or real measurement.
- School rules on AI use differ. Please check with the school if you are unsure.

Project ideas and a report template are in [`projects/`](../projects/README.md).
