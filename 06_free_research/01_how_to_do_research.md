# 6-1. How to do research — seven steps to a fair answer

A good free research project is not about getting a "correct" result. It is about
asking a clear question, testing it **fairly**, and telling **honestly** what you found.
This page shows the steps, the rules for a fair test, how to treat people fairly, and
how to write about your AI use.

## The seven steps

1. **Question**: What do you want to know? Make it specific.
   ✗ "Is Wi-Fi interesting?" ✓ "Does a person 3 m away change CSI less than a person 1 m away?"
2. **Hypothesis**: What do you think will happen, and **why**?
3. **Plan**: What will you change? What will you keep the same? How many times will you repeat?
4. **Measure**: Record data with the boards ([Chapter 3](../03_build/README.md)) or make it
   with the simulator. Write down the conditions every time.
5. **Analyze**: Make graphs and numbers ([Chapter 4](../04_analysis/README.md) and
   [Chapter 5](../05_machine_learning/README.md)).
6. **Conclude**: Was your hypothesis right, wrong, or "not sure yet"? All three are OK.
7. **Reflect**: What would you do differently? What new question did you find?

Write everything in the [research sheet](research_sheet.md) as you go, not only at the end.

## Tips for a fair test

- **Change only one thing at a time.** If you change the distance *and* the room, you
  can't know which one made the difference.
- **Repeat at least 3 times** for each condition. One recording can be a lucky (or
  unlucky) one. Report the average and how much the results spread.
- **Record the conditions** every time: date and time, distance between the boards,
  height, what was between them, how many people were in the room, the file name.
- **Keep a "nothing moves" recording** in every session. It is your baseline for the
  threshold ([4-2](../04_analysis/02_threshold.md)).
- **Don't delete "bad" data** just because it doesn't match your hypothesis. If
  something went wrong (someone walked in), write it down instead.

## Being fair to people (ethics)

- **Ask first.** Only record people who agreed, and tell them what you are measuring.
  Wi-Fi sensing works through walls, so never measure people who don't know about it.
- **No names in files or reports.** Use "Person A", "Person B".
- **Don't share recordings of your home** on the internet. Share graphs and numbers instead.
- Ask a parent or teacher to check your plan before you start.
- More on safety and privacy: [Safety](../start_here/04_safety.md).

## Reporting how you used AI

Using an AI study buddy is fine. Hiding it is not. In your report, write:

- **What the AI helped with** (e.g. "explained standard deviation", "found a bug in my code").
- **What you checked yourself** (e.g. "I ran the code and compared it with my own graph").
- **What you decided yourself** (your question, your hypothesis, your conclusion).
- If the AI said something wrong, write that too. Finding it is a skill!

Also ask your school whether they have rules about using AI for free research.
More tips: [Learning with AI](../start_here/03_learning_with_ai.md).

> 🤖 **Ask your AI**
> - "I'm in grade 7. Here is my research question: [your question]. Help me make it more specific and testable. Ask me questions instead of rewriting it for me."
> - "Here is my plan: [your plan]. What might make my test unfair? Give me hints, not a new plan."
